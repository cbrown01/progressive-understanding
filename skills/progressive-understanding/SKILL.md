# Progressive Understanding

Explain the material so understanding accumulates. A reader may stop after any section and still hold a true, useful picture. Each section adds something the earlier sections did not. Do not use an idea before it has been introduced.

Apply this to anything someone wants to understand—a concept, object, process, event, decision, story, or body of work. This includes technical material such as plans, pull requests, code under review, and repositories.

## Order

1. **Idea** — One sentence: what this is, or why it exists. For a plan or PR, this is the Goal. For a topic, this is the definition.

2. **ELI5** — 1–3 sentences in plain language. Build the intuition: how to think about this. This is the foundation for everything below. Plain, not childish.

3. **TLDR** — Up to 5 bullets. Factual, direct, and a step more technical than the ELI5. Build on the intuition; do not restate it. This is what to know if the reader stops here.

4. **Visual** — A diagram or other quick visualization, when one would help. Omit the section when it would not.

5. **You Should Know** — Up to 5 facts that deepen the picture: how the subject works, behaves, or exists, including gotchas and landmines.

6. **This, Not That** — Up to 5 contrasts that sharpen the picture. A decision (we made it a class, not a function, because…) or a distinction (bread is not pasta, because…). Omit the section when there is no real contrast.

7. **Example** — One concrete walkthrough of the subject in action. Omit the section when the material is already an example.

8. **Deep Dive** — The fullest account, still easy to read. Use headings and short paragraphs. State what the dive covers and what it leaves out. No walls of text.

## Rules

- ELI5 answers "how should I think about this?" TLDR answers "what do I need to know?"
- Later sections build on earlier ones. They do not repeat them.
- Omit a section that adds nothing. Keep its place empty rather than padding it.
- Put an open question or uncertainty in the earliest section where the reader can understand why it matters.
- Match terms to the material. Do not rename the subject's own words to fit these headings.
