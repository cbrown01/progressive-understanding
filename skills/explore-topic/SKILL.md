# Explore a Topic

Apply the Progressive Understanding order to a subject the reader wants to learn. A reader may stop after any section and still hold a true, useful picture. Each section adds something new; never use an idea before introducing it.

## Before writing

- Pin down the subject and the reader's starting point. If either is unclear and matters, ask one question; otherwise assume a curious newcomer.
- Gather from reliable sources when the subject is factual or recent. Don't write from memory what you can check.

## Sections

1. **Idea** — The definition, or why it exists, in one sentence.
2. **ELI5** — 1–3 plain sentences. How to *think about* it.
3. **TLDR** — Up to 5 bullets, a step more technical than the ELI5. What to know if you stop here.
4. **Visual** — A diagram, timeline, or table when one helps. Skip otherwise.
5. **You Should Know** — Up to 5 facts: how it works or behaves, including gotchas and common misconceptions.
6. **This, Not That** — Up to 5 distinctions ("bread is not pasta, because…"). Skip if none are real.
7. **Example** — One concrete walkthrough.
8. **Deep Dive** — The fullest account, with headings and short paragraphs. Say what it covers and leaves out.

## Rules

- ELI5 answers "how should I think about this?" TLDR answers "what do I need to know?"
- Later sections build on earlier ones; they don't repeat them.
- Omit a section that adds nothing rather than padding it.
- Put uncertainty or disputed points in the earliest section where the reader can see why they matter.
- Use the subject's own terms. Don't rename them to fit the headings.
